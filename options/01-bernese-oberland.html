Bernese Oberland

The Valley of 72 Waterfalls

Cliffs, waterfalls and the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau outside your window. The classic Swiss Alps, done from one cosy base.

At a glance

Why this trip

All lifts open from mid-June. Late May has the biggest waterfalls, but trails above 1,800 m are still snowy.

  • 1Iconic Switzerland: Lauterbrunnen's 300 m Staubbach Falls, car-free Mürren and the Eiger north face.
  • 2One base for the whole trip means no packing and repacking. Every day trip is under 1 hour away.
  • 3Something for every energy level, from flat valley strolls to the famous Bachalpsee and Oeschinensee lakes.
  • 4Easy by train from Langenfeld, with one or two changes and no car needed.

The plan

Day by day

Keep 1–2 days flexible and save the big mountain days for clear weather.

Day1

Arrive in the valley

Langenfeld → Köln → Basel → Interlaken → Lauterbrunnen. Check in and stroll to Staubbach Falls at golden hour.

Shop at the Coop in Lauterbrunnen or Interlaken for the first days.
Day2

Mürren & Gimmelwald

Cable car to Grütschalp, then the flat panorama path to car-free Mürren (≈ 1.5 h) with the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau ahead. Walk down to the hamlet of Gimmelwald and take the cable car to Stechelberg.

Picnic on the Mürren meadows; Allmendhubel has a short flower trail.
Day3

Trümmelbach & Sefinental

Morning at Trümmelbach Falls, 10 glacier waterfalls inside the mountain (≈ CHF 16). Afternoon in the quiet Sefinental valley from Stechelberg, a hidden gem most people skip.

Optional splurge: Schilthorn / Piz Gloria (≈ CHF 115 return, half with Half Fare).
Day4

Grindelwald First

Train to Grindelwald, gondola to First. First Cliff Walk, then hike to the Bachalpsee mirror lake (≈ 1 h each way) for the Schreckhorn reflected in still water.

Go early for calm water. In late May / early June the trail can still be snowy.
Day5

Royal Walk & Kleine Scheidegg

Cable car from Wengen to Männlichen, then the easy Panorama Trail to Kleine Scheidegg (≈ 1.5 h) under the Eiger. Train down through Wengen.

Splurge option: carry on to the Jungfraujoch ('Top of Europe') on the clearest day of your stay.
Day6

Lake Brienz day

Turquoise Lake Brienz: bus to Iseltwald (the pier costs CHF 5), boat to Giessbach Falls and the old funicular, then Brienz village. Harder Kulm above Interlaken at sunset.

The Aare river in Interlaken glows turquoise, so walk along it in the evening.
Day7

Oeschinensee & Blausee

Train to Kandersteg, gondola (CHF 36–40 return) and a 20-minute walk to Oeschinensee, the most beautiful lake in the Alps. Fit hikers take the higher Heuberg panorama trail. On the way back, stop at the tiny Blausee (CHF 11–13).

Rowing boats on Oeschinensee in summer, and the Berghaus serves rösti with a view.
Day8

Schynige Platte or Thun

From mid-June: vintage cog railway to Schynige Platte (alpine garden, panorama loop ≈ 2.5 h). Rainy-day plan: Thun old town and castle, Oberhofen castle by boat, or the St. Beatus caves.

In 2026 the Schynige Platte railway opened on 13 June.
Day9

Home

Late breakfast, then the train back to Langenfeld (≈ 7 h).

Getting there & around

From your door to the mountains

Langenfeld→Köln / Düsseldorf→Basel→Lauterbrunnen

Getting there

Langenfeld → Köln Hbf (S6, 20 min) → ICE to Basel SBB (≈ 4 h) → IC to Interlaken Ost (≈ 2 h) → Bernese Oberland Railway (BOB) to Lauterbrunnen (20 min). Book one through-ticket Köln → Lauterbrunnen with DB 'Sparpreis Europa', usually €40–80 each way if booked when sales open.

  • 💡 International DB tickets open 6 months ahead. Buy all 5 seats in one booking on day one.
  • 💡 'Super Sparpreis' can't be refunded. 'Sparpreis' can be cancelled before travel for a small fee.
  • 💡 Domestic tickets to Basel Bad Bf open 12 months ahead and are often cheapest. Basel SBB is one stop on.
  • 💡 DB group fares start at 6 people, and a BahnCard only discounts the German part.

Getting around

Berner Oberland Pass, 6 days: CHF 350, or CHF 254 if you are under 25 or hold a Half Fare Card. It covers trains, buses and lake boats, plus 25+ mountain lifts (Schynige Platte, Männlichen and more), with big discounts on others (e.g. First at 50 %). If the Jungfraujoch is a must, take the Jungfrau Travel Pass + Jungfraujoch add-on (CHF 89) instead. That is the cheapest way to the top.

Before you go

Good to know & worth the splurge

Good to know

  • Lauterbrunnen gets day-tripper crowds from about 10 am to 4 pm, so start early. The town has discussed a visitor fee, so check before you go.
  • Keep 2–3 days flexible and do the mountain days when the forecast is clear (MeteoSwiss app and the lift webcams).
  • Snow lingers above ~1,800 m until mid or late June: Bachalpsee, Faulhorn and Schynige Platte may be partly closed in late May.
  • Shops close on Sundays, except those at Interlaken Ost station.

Worth paying for

  • Jungfraujoch≈ CHF 131 with Half Fare, or JTP + CHF 89Glacier views at 3,454 m. Only worth it on a crystal-clear day, so buy 1–2 days ahead.
  • Tandem paragliding≈ CHF 170–200Land in Interlaken after floating between two lakes and the Jungfrau.
  • Breakfast at Schilthorn≈ CHF 60 + liftA 360° view of 200 peaks from the James Bond revolving restaurant.
