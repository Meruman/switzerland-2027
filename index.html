If we had to choose

For a first trip with 5 people who love nature and want to keep costs down, our favourite is still mid to late June 2027 using one of the 14-day combos below. When to go

May, June, July or August?

Late May to June is the best value. July and August are the best months for the high Alps, but they are peak season, so you pay more and share the views.

May cheaper · greener · snow up high

Shoulder season: lower prices, fewer tourists, waterfalls at their peak and blossoming meadows. But many high trails above ~1,800 m are still snowy and some lifts are closed for maintenance. Best regions: Ticino, Lake Geneva, Lucerne, Appenzell, Italian lakes. Watch out for the long weekends (Ascension 6 May, Pentecost 16–17 May), when Swiss and Germans travel too.

June everything opens

From mid-June nearly all lifts and passes are open, the days are the longest of the year and the high-alpine lakes thaw. It is still before the July–August school holidays. Sweet spot: Sat 12 – Sat 26 June 2027. Late June is ideal for Zermatt, Engadin and the road trip.

July all open · peak season starts

Every lift, pass and high trail is open, alpine flowers peak in early July and the lakes get warm enough to swim. But Swiss schools break up around 3 July and NRW's on 19 July, so prices climb and the famous spots are busy. Montreux Jazz Festival runs 2–17 July. Best regions: Valais, Engadin, Appenzell and the road trip. Summer sweet spot: 1–16 July, before the German holidays.

August warmest · busiest · priciest

The warmest lakes and reliable high-alpine hiking, plus Swiss National Day on Sunday 1 August. It is also the most crowded month: Italians fill Ticino and Lake Como around Ferragosto (15 Aug), and beds are at peak prices. It gets better after ~16 August, when most Swiss schools go back. Avoid the Italian lakes and Ticino in mid-August.

Is July–August worth it?

Why it is great ✓

  • Every lift, pass, hut and high trail is open: no snow surprises on the Five Lakes Walk, Faulhorn, Aletsch or the Furka and Susten passes.
  • Warm lakes and rivers for swimming (≈ 20–24 °C in Lake Geneva, Lugano, Thun and Brienz).
  • Alpine flowers peak in early July, and the evenings are long and warm for picnics, BBQ spots and open-air events.
  • Swiss National Day on 1 August: bonfires on the hills, fireworks over the lakes and farm brunches.
  • Swiss passes (Swiss Travel Pass, Half Fare, regional passes) cost the same as in June.

What you pay for it ✗

  • Accommodation costs ≈ 10–35 % more and sells out earlier. Booking sites show July as Lauterbrunnen's most expensive month.
  • Crowds and midday queues at the hotspots: Lauterbrunnen, Grindelwald, Lucerne, Zermatt, Oeschinensee, Lake Como.
  • Heat waves (30–35 °C in Ticino, Valais and around Geneva in recent summers) and almost daily afternoon thunderstorms in the mountains.
  • Packed DB trains in the NRW holidays (from 19 July). The cheapest Sparpreis fares go faster.
  • Dry summers can bring fire bans, so check before lighting a BBQ.
💶 In Switzerland, July–August costs ≈ 4–13 % more: about €30–370 more per person, depending on the option and comfort level. Appenzell & the East changes least (+4 %) and Swiss–Italian Lakes most (+13 %). On top of that, the flights from Mexico City cost ≈ €240–350 more per person, and the train from Langenfeld ≈ 20 % more. Each option's 'Month by month' slide has the exact numbers.
Getting there

Two routes, one meeting point

You two come from Langenfeld by train (or by car for the road trip). The three friends fly in from Mexico City, and everyone meets at the first base or at the airport station.

You two: Langenfeld → Switzerland

Langenfeld sits on the S6 between Köln and Düsseldorf, which have direct ICEs to Basel. The train is the best balance of price, comfort and time. A car wins only for the road trip.

🚆
Train (recommended)5–8 h
€30–80 pp each way if booked early

Langenfeld (Rheinl) → Köln or Düsseldorf Hbf (S6/RE, 15–20 min) → ICE to Basel SBB (≈ 4 h) → anywhere in Switzerland. Book DB 'Sparpreis Europa' / 'Super Sparpreis Europa' (from €19.99–24.99). International tickets open 6 months ahead. Domestic tickets to Basel Bad Bf (the German station in Basel) open 12 months ahead and are often cheapest. From there it is a 5-minute hop to Basel SBB.

🚗
Car6–7 h
≈ €20–60 pp each way (fuel ÷ 5) + CHF 40 vignette

Best for the Road Trip (Option 09): drive down and pick the friends up at Zürich airport. Five adults with two weeks of luggage need a 7-seater or a roof box. Buy the e-vignette at via.admin.ch only (CHF 40; the 2027 vignette is valid 1 Dec 2026 – 31 Jan 2028). Parking at the car-free resorts costs ≈ CHF 10–25/day.

🚌
FlixBus9–11 h
≈ €25–40 pp

Köln → Zürich/Basel. The cheapest ticket, but you lose most of a day each way. The train is usually worth the extra €10–30.

✈️
Flights≈ 13–16 h with one stop

There is no nonstop flight to Switzerland yet (Swiss has only talked about one). The usual routes have one stop: Madrid (Aeroméxico, Iberia), Paris (Air France), Amsterdam (KLM), Frankfurt or Munich (Lufthansa) or London (British Airways). 🛬
Which airportZürich · Geneva · Milan
pick the one closest to your first base

Zürich (ZRH) has the most connections and a station under the terminal: Lucerne ≈ 1 h, Bern ≈ 1 h 15, Interlaken ≈ 2 h 15. Geneva (GVA) suits Lake Geneva and Valais. Milan Malpensa (MXP) suits Ticino and the Italian lakes, ≈ 1 h 30 from Lugano. Fly home from a different airport (an open-jaw ticket) when the trip ends somewhere else, to skip a long train ride back.

🛂
Entry & documentsMexican passport
no visa for up to 90 days

Mexicans need no visa for Switzerland and the Schengen area for up to 90 days in any 180. The passport must be valid at least 3 months after you leave. Fingerprints and a photo are taken at the first border under the EU's Entry/Exit System (fully running since April 2026). ETIAS, the €20 online travel authorisation, has not started yet; the latest reports say 2027 at the earliest, with a 6-month transition. Check before you fly. Travel insurance with medical cover and mountain rescue is strongly recommended.

🤝
Meeting up & jet lagMexico City is 8 h behind Swiss summer time
plan an easy first day

Flights from Mexico are overnight and land in Europe in the morning or afternoon. Meet at Zürich airport station, which you two reach from Langenfeld in ≈ 5.5–6 h, or go straight to the first base and meet there. Stay up until the evening and save the big mountain day for day 3, once everyone has slept.

Booking timeline

How far ahead to book

Today is 25 October 2026. Accommodation in the hotspots is the thing to secure first, with free cancellation. Train tickets get more expensive the closer you get. Mountain tickets should be bought last, once you see the weather.

Now (late Oct – Nov 2026)Vote on an option and dates. Book refundable accommodation for hotspots (Lauterbrunnen, Täsch/Zermatt, Lucerne, Lake Como). For July–August this matters even more, because peak-season beds go first. The friends set Google Flights alerts for Mexico City → Zürich, Geneva and Milan. Practical

Good to know before you go

🔌 PlugsSwiss sockets (type J) take thin 2-pin Euro plugs but NOT German Schuko plugs or Mexican flat-pin plugs. Bring adapters. Mexico runs on 127 V: phone and laptop chargers are fine, but check hair dryers and straighteners.
📱 RoamingSwitzerland is not in the EU roaming zone, and Mexican plans can be expensive abroad. Check your tariff or buy a Swiss or Europe eSIM data plan.
💳 MoneyCHF. Cards and phones work almost everywhere: always pay in francs on card terminals and use a no-fee card. Friends: enable international use on Mexican cards. Keep some cash for mountain huts.
🏥 HealthThe EHIC card covers the two of you (German insurance). The friends from Mexico need travel insurance with medical cover and mountain rescue.
⛈️ WeatherJune to August afternoons bring thunderstorms, so start hikes early. July–August heat waves can hit 30–35 °C in the valleys. Check MeteoSwiss and the webcams before buying lift tickets.
🥾 TrailsYellow signs = easy paths. White-red-white = mountain trails (sure-footed). Blue-white = alpine (not for you).
🛒 SundaysShops close on Sundays, except at train stations and petrol stations.
🧳 LuggageSBB luggage forwarding (≈ CHF 12–25 per bag) lets you hop between bases with only a daypack.
🛂 DocumentsSchengen rules apply. Mexicans need no visa for up to 90 days; fingerprints and a photo are taken at the first border (EES). ETIAS (€20 online) is not running yet and is expected in 2027 at the earliest, so re-check before travel.
